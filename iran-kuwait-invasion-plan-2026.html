Iran Considers Ground Invasion of Kuwait to Seize US Military Bases and Capture American Soldiers

Senior Iranian analysts and officials signal that Tehran is seriously examining a ground incursion into Kuwait targeting Camp Buehring, Camp Arifjan and Ali Al Salem Air Base. Former FM Mottaki warned Iran could take US soldiers as prisoners of war. Analysts warn such a move would amount to a de facto regional world war.

July 19, 2026 Kuwait · Tehran 5 min read

A ground war option emerges

The Middle East crisis is veering towards full-scale regional war. Following days of intensifying US air strikes and Iranian missile and drone retaliation, reports have emerged that Tehran is seriously considering a ground incursion into Kuwait — a move that would represent a dramatic and potentially irreversible escalation of the conflict.

The disclosure, based on statements from senior Iranian analysts and officials, makes clear that if Washington continues its military campaign against Iran, Tehran's response will shift from retaliatory strikes to a generalised, full-scale offensive. The primary targets would be Camp Buehring and Camp Arifjan — the two principal US Army logistics and staging hubs in Kuwait — and Ali Al Salem Air Base, a key US Air Force forward operating base.

Kuwait is considered one of Iran's primary options for a potential ground invasion, with the goal of seizing the two American military bases located within the country. — Mehdi Hanalizadeh, Iranian analyst

Capturing American soldiers

The threat extends beyond base seizure. Former Iranian Foreign Minister Manouchehr Mottaki has warned that Iran possesses the capability to capture American soldiers and transfer them to Iranian territory as prisoners of war. Iranian officials are signalling that no political border will be considered safe, and the country will shift to a full-scale offensive strategy transforming the Persian Gulf into an active war zone.

Historical echoes and strategic implications

If such a scenario were to materialise, it would be the second time in 36 years that Kuwait has suffered a ground invasion — after Saddam Hussein's Iraqi invasion of August 1990. Defence analysts warn that an Iranian ground incursion into Kuwait would constitute a de facto declaration of regional world war, compelling the United States and its allies into a massive military response, permanently closing the Strait of Hormuz and devastating the global economy. The IRGC has amplified the narrative on social media, posting that Kuwait's suffering is a direct consequence of hosting US bases — framing clearly designed to provide a pretext for military action against a sovereign Gulf state.
